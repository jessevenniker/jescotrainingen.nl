Every question you put to ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini is answered in a data centre. Chips run on electricity there, and those chips need cooling, often with water. So what does it actually cost? And should your team be worried about it?

## Per prompt: not much

For a long time there were only outside estimates. In August 2025, Google published its own figures for the first time (Google, 2025). In May 2025, a typical text prompt to Gemini used about 0.24 watt-hours of electricity and 0.26 millilitres of water. That's roughly nine seconds of watching TV and five drops of water. Google puts the emissions at 0.03 grams of CO₂.

Google also reports that energy use per prompt fell more than thirtyfold in a year, thanks to more efficient chips, models and data centres.

There are some caveats:

- It's Google's **own measurement**, not independently verified.
- It covers the **typical text prompt**. Long prompts, images and video generally take far more computing power.
- Critics point out that not everything was counted, such as the water used to generate electricity and the manufacturing of the chips themselves.

## Added up: a lot

The cost per prompt says little without the scale. AI is now used billions of times a day. The International Energy Agency (IEA, 2025) calculated that data centres used around 415 terawatt-hours in 2024, about 1.5 percent of all electricity worldwide. By 2030 that's expected to roughly double to about 945 terawatt-hours, more than all of Japan uses today. AI is the main driver.

The IEA stresses that a lot is uncertain: how quickly AI is adopted, how efficient the technology becomes and whether power grids can keep up. But the direction is clear.

## What does this mean for your team?

There's no need to feel guilty about asking AI a question. Per person, the footprint is small. But it pays to choose consciously, and that lines up well with other good reasons to do so.

**Not every task needs AI.** A three-sentence email is often quicker to write yourself. Using AI only where it really adds something saves electricity, and time and checking too.

**One good prompt instead of five.** A prompt with a role, context and clear limits often gets a usable answer first time. That saves regenerating.

**Go easy on images and video.** A picture made for fun takes far more computing power than a text prompt.

**Avoid workslop.** Work that looks polished but adds little costs the recipient time (Niederhoffer et al., 2025). It also took electricity to produce. Whoever sends it, owns it.

## Mindful use is good use

What stands out: the habits that are better for the environment are the same habits that are better for your work and your head. Choosing when to use AI, asking good questions, and not passing everything on unchecked. In the training, this comes together in the agreements your team makes, including when you deliberately don't use AI.

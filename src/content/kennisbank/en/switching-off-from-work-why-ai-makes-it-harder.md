You close your laptop, but your head is still at work. While cooking, you think of a better way to get that proposal drafted. On the sofa you grab your phone to ask ChatGPT something "quickly". Before you know it, you're working again, without ever meaning to.

## What switching off means

Psychologists distinguish four experiences that help people recover from work: mentally detaching, relaxing, doing something challenging outside work, and being in control of your free time (Sonnentag & Fritz, 2007). Mental detachment means that after work you not only stop doing work, you also stop thinking about it.

That's more than a nice feeling. A meta-analysis of research on detachment shows it's linked to less fatigue and exhaustion and to greater wellbeing (Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah, 2017).

## Why AI makes it harder

Letting go of work has never been easy, but AI adds something new:

- **Work is always one prompt away.** AI tools live on your phone and laptop. An idea is quickly fleshed out, even at ten at night.
- **Small tasks don't feel like work.** Getting an email rewritten "real quick" feels light, but your mind still switches back to work mode.
- **Unfinished tasks keep nagging.** Especially when you know AI could pick them up in no time, your mind keeps returning to them.

## What helps

The good news: switching off is a habit you can practise.

**Write down what's still open.** Unfinished tasks linger in your mind. Making a concrete plan, with a first step for tomorrow, takes away much of that restlessness (Masicampo & Baumeister, 2011). Five minutes at the end of the day is enough.

**Make if-then plans.** A plan in the form "if this happens, then I'll do that" makes the behaviour you want more automatic (Gollwitzer, 1999). For example: "If I get a work idea in the evening, then I'll put it in my notes and won't open an AI tool."

**Use a fixed closing gesture.** Laptop shut, coat on, a quick walk outside. A fixed moment helps your mind understand that the working day is over.

**Turn off notifications.** Work apps and AI tools don't need anything from you after hours.

## What your team can do

1. **Agree an end time for AI work and messages.** If nobody sends messages after 6 pm, switching off gets easier for everyone.
2. **Batch AI work during the day.** People who do AI work in fixed blocks take fewer loose ends home with them.
3. **Make it something you talk about.** Ask in a team meeting how people manage to let go of work. You'll often find more people are struggling with it than you thought.
4. **Lead by example.** Managers who don't send messages in the evening make it much easier for their team.

## A caveat

Most research on detachment was done before generative AI became commonplace. That AI makes it harder is a logical step from what we know, not a separately measured effect. The meta-analysis also mainly shows associations: people who detach better feel better, but that doesn't automatically mean one causes the other. The advice is simple, safe and easy to try, though.
